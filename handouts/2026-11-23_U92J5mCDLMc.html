40x More Merged PRs With 2.7x the Engineers — Walden Yan, CPO, Cognition

AI Engineer · 14 min talk · Watch · Transcript · 2026-11-23 13:28 UTC · gemini-3.6-flash

Cognition attributes its 40x delivery leap to changing how it builds - agent-testable infrastructure, vertical pods, and outcome-level delegation - rather than to the coding agent alone.

TL;DR

  • Merged PRs grew 40.4x in about a year on 2.7x R&D headcount; the unlock is rebuilding how work runs, not agents alone.
  • The hard prerequisite is an agent-testable codebase - all services mocked and locally runnable - not the coding agent purchase itself.
  • Teams restructured into 2-3 engineer vertical pods owning product, QA, and oncall end to end, with agent swarms implementing.
  • Delegate outcome-level jobs with KPIs and budgets rather than tasks; analyzers over logged sessions then compound the gains.

Walden Yan, co-founder and CPO of Cognition (maker of the Devin coding agent), spoke at the AI Engineer Code Summit. All evidence is self-reported internal telemetry from one company: PR throughput over Oct 2025-Oct 2026, a roughly one-week GPU tuning run, and a 44-day Android build. No methodology, baselines, or third-party validation appear; the vendor measures its own agent, and quality claims are assertions.

Deep dives

[04:24] Agent-testable codebase

  • Testing moved from cloud staging, staging databases, and live servers to everything running locally via Docker Compose inside one VM.
  • Every third-party service has a mocked local version; no code agent touches live services to test changes.
  • VMs rebuild hourly from latest code; Devins repair failed snapshot builds, self-maintain SKILL.md testing skills; multi-account Zoom voice tests ran in one VM.

Why it matters: Reproducible, isolated, from-scratch environments make agent verification cheap and trustworthy; the migration, not buying agents, is the stated hard part. (inference)

Watch out: Mock fidelity drifts from live third parties; hourly rebuilds cost compute; migration effort for legacy estates is unaddressed.

[03:30] Slack as the delivery and proof surface

  • Customer bug reports arrive as Slack videos and are forwarded to an internal channel, tagging Devin.
  • Devin posts a PR with CI passing plus a recorded proof, shared straight back to the customer.
  • Stated end goal: run the lifecycle without opening an IDE or GitHub; some engineers develop from iPads.

Why it matters: Recorded proofs make agent output auditable by non-engineers and close the customer loop in one tool. (inference)

Watch out: Videos prove only the demonstrated scenarios; regressions outside them stay invisible.

[08:24] Jobs, not tasks

  • Give agents measurable outcomes to work toward over long durations; stop prompting stepwise tasks.
  • The agent itself adds telemetry, alerts, weekly reports, and decomposes the goal into issues.
  • Anything considered unmeasurable should first be made measurable.

Why it matters: KPI, budget, and alert contracts are the control surface for long-running autonomy; judgment shifts to defining and checking outcomes. (inference)

Watch out: A self-defined KPI can miss the real goal; no failure or rollback case is shown.

[02:14] Vertical pods with agent swarms

  • Large role-based teams were split into pods of two-three engineers, each given agent swarms.
  • Pods own one vertical end to end: customer bug intake, QA, product management, their own Linear board.
  • Anything described and implementable goes to the agent; ticket-picking alone is not set up for success.

Why it matters: End-to-end ownership removes cross-function handoffs so delegated work lands faster; scarce skills become scoping and verification. (inference)

Watch out: No metrics isolate this org change; tiny pods concentrate oncall load and knowledge risk.

Field playbook (inferred from the talk, not claims made in it)

Ask the customer

  • Which third-party services block local verification, and who will build and maintain their mocks?
  • What share of current PRs could merge with no human approval, and which checks genuinely gate that?
  • Which outcomes are already measured continuously, and what needs instrumenting before delegation?
  • Could one channel carry bug reports, agent proofs, and approvals end to end?

Measure in a pilot

  • Merged PRs per human message, plus zero-message days, over a fixed window.
  • Share of changes verified in the isolated environment before any human review.
  • Session cost and test time before and after scoping CI to the change.

Where it breaks

  • Mocks drift from live third-party behavior; locally verified changes fail in production.
  • The job optimizes its stated KPI, not the intended outcome; errors compound at high PR volume.
  • Legacy services resist containerization; the local-first migration stalls before the factory closes.

What the talk does not establish

  • No methodology behind the throughput multiple: PR size, complexity, and rejection rates are unreported.
  • Quality parity with a year ago is asserted; no defect, rollback, or incident data appears.
  • No before-and-after baselines isolate the migration or pod restructure effects; all gains are attributed jointly.
  • Escalation volume and human review time in the default-autonomous pipeline are never quantified.

Checklist

  • Inventory services blocking local verification; build mocked Docker Compose substitutes for each. [04:56]
  • Stand up per-agent VMs rebuilt hourly from latest code; let agents repair failed builds. [05:08]
  • Route customer bug reports into one channel where the agent posts PRs with recorded proof. [03:41]
  • Recast one recurring pain as a job: set KPI, budget, alert threshold, and weekly report first. [08:24]
  • Run an analyzer over logged agent sessions for wasted time, tokens, and flaky suites. [07:25]
  • Count merged PRs per human message and zero-message days before reporting autonomy gains. [13:08]
