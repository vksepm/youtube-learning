How 21 People Built a $100M+ Agent Company — Quinn Slack, Amp

AI Engineer · 18 min talk · Watch · Transcript · 2026-11-26 14:07 UTC · gemini-4-hydrogen

He argues agents invert company design: when agents prove correctness and run operations, reviews, meetings, PMs, and SaaS fall away — explaining how a tiny company outships large ones.

TL;DR

  • Amp reports >$100M ARR, profitable, on 21 people — 1/10 to 1/30 competitor headcount — after an 8-month spin-out blocked hiring.
  • Risk shifts from human review to agent-generated proof: small pushes, shadow systems, and checks no human has patience for.
  • Support runs as one agent loop — investigate, human-approved apology emails with credits, find everyone affected in logs.
  • Twelve internal tools run as plugins and MCP servers in every thread; he argues the SaaS behind them becomes unnecessary.

Talk by Quinn Slack, CEO of Amp, a coding-agent product spun out of Sourcegraph, at the AI Engineer Code Summit. All evidence is self-reported: internal deploy, dictation, and shipping charts, real Amp thread screenshots, Slack messages, and tweets. Deep dives

[07:00] Threads are the only work surface

  • All other tools were dropped — not Jira, no task tracking, no support system; even communication happens in Amp threads.
  • The agent listens, summarizes, and starts no work unless asked; a request spawns work in the thread.
  • A local laptop agent cannot pull someone in when you are in over your head; shared threads can.

Why it matters: One agent-visible surface removes handoffs and makes every remark actionable. (inference)

Watch out: Coordination lives in one vendor's platform; audit and export depend on it.

[07:28] Risk without code review

  • Slide quote: the code review process is what creates the risk.
  • Chunk work small: add logging first to confirm understanding, push, let the agent monitor, push again.
  • Run a shadow system side-by-side with the old one to verify identical results in every case.

Why it matters: Safety moves from approval queues to per-push verification and reversibility. (inference)

Watch out: Shadow comparison assumes the old system still runs and outputs are deterministic.

[08:38] Irrefutable proof on demand

  • Agents are really good at proving correctness; ask for irrefutable proof of what they built.
  • Example: the agent extracted frames at 10 fps and matched the 32px icon across a 7px search area.
  • Zero movement across 56- and 59-frame recordings; it also fixed a second source, 40px vs 20px reserved heights.

Why it matters: Verification cost collapses, so acceptance can be mechanical per change. (inference)

Watch out: Frame matching proves visual stability only, not logic or data correctness.

[09:06] Ask for many versions, do not iterate

  • Do not request one small change at a time; have the agent make many variants at once.
  • Waveform example: 31 commands, 4 files edited, animated options presented in a Storybook; he chose one, deleted the rest.

Why it matters: Parallel exploration converts agent wait time into throughput; framed as the cure for waiting on your agent. (inference)

Watch out: Variant explosion multiplies token spend and human selection load.

Field playbook (inferred from the talk, not claims made in it)

Ask the customer

  • Which approval step actually catches defects today, and what evidence does it produce?
  • What share of each commercial SaaS tool does the team actually use?
  • Who touches a bug between report and customer notification, and how long?

Measure in a pilot

  • Median push-to-production and deploys per day with agent proof replacing review.
  • Share of agent changes accepted by mechanical checks, not opinion.
  • Bug-report to customer-notification latency and credits issued per incident.

Where it breaks

  • Non-deterministic outputs defeat shadow comparison; identical-results checks become meaningless.
  • Daily-change cadence collides with audit and compliance built for scheduled releases.
  • The human email-approval gate bottlenecks when bug volume spikes.

What the talk does not establish

  • No incident, failure, or revert rates accompany the daily deploy cadence.
  • Headline business figures lack methodology, period, or independent verification.
  • 'No token limits' is asserted with no token-spend or compute-cost data.
  • Competitor lines on the headcount chart are unlabeled and unverifiable.

Checklist

  • Replace one review-gated change with small agent-proved pushes, starting with logging. [07:50]
  • Run a shadow system beside the old path and compare results on every case. [08:00]
  • Require mechanical verification, like frame or output comparison, for one agent-delivered fix. [08:38]
  • Request several full variants of one component in a single agent ask. [09:06]
  • Gate every agent-drafted outbound customer message behind human approval. [10:07]
  • Map the roughly-2% feature subset your team uses in each SaaS tool before renewal. [12:20]
